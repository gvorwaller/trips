# 2026-08-01 — Suggestion ranking by driving time, place dates, day-plan duplication

Three tracker items closed and deployed, mid-trip in coastal Maine. Shipped as
`78b4069` (td-65f48c) and `de004bc` (td-e00086 + td-5e5e46).

## Why this happened at all

The trigger was the owner using the app on the trip and finding "Suggest stops"
useless: dozens of places at varying distances from the day's route, most
irrelevant, in no order. The stated ask was "sort by proximity to other stops."
The actual want, in their words, was places that *extend the line out from base*,
sit *a short way off it*, or *fit the loop* — anything that doesn't mean driving
all over the region on bad roads.

Those three shapes are one metric: **how much extra driving does adding this
place cost?** Not "how far is it from something."

## Cloning prod to test was the turning point

`scripts/copy-prod-to-test.sh` already existed. Cloning the real trip (95 places,
94 located, 7 day plans, all anchored at Blue Hill, spread over ~220 × 330 km
into New Brunswick and Nova Scotia) and scoring it turned a plausible design into
a measured one — and killed two ideas that had seemed obviously right:

- **The failure was false positives, not hidden gems.** Zero genuinely-cheap
  places were being excluded by the old 30 km centroid circle. It was showing
  ~5× too many and not ordering them. An earlier draft proposed fanning the
  nearby-places lookup out to 4 sample points to widen the net; the data said
  that would add noise and API spend for nothing. Dropped.
- **My measured table was wrong the first time.** The analysis forgot that
  `dayplan-suggest` excludes places already in the plan. Peer review caught it;
  re-running with the exclusion gave 64 candidates of which only 13 were cheap —
  worse than originally reported, not better.

## What the maths actually says

A sideways offset *h* from a leg of length *L* costs about **2h²/L**, not 2h.
Two km off an 11 km leg costs 0.7 km. The "twice the offset" intuition — which
this module was first specified with — is wrong by ~5× at typical scale, and only
holds when *h* approaches *L*. A unit test caught it. Physically right: a small
diversion off a long drive is nearly free.

## Straight-line distance cannot rank this trip

The design originally shortlisted candidates by straight-line detour and then
refined the top few with real road times. Peer review refuted it with live road
data, and the repo data confirmed:

- It **refines the wrong edge**. Straight-line put Southern Bay Rd on the closing
  edge; by road it belongs between Caterpillar Hill Overlook and Compass Rose
  Books. Fixing the edge from straight-line means the "real driving" stage never
  computes cheapest road insertion at all — it prices one arbitrary slot.
- It **cuts the wrong candidates**. On Scenic loop, every candidate from rank 5
  to rank 27 scores under 1 km. That band is below the method's own error, because
  the chord between two stops is not the road. A 12-item shortlist would have
  discarded Blue Hill Books, Humblebee Cafe, Brooklin Village, Hatch Cove
  Preserve, the Farmers Market and Southern Bay Rd — while keeping three
  cemeteries.

So: road-score everything against every edge, before any cap.

## Google Routes API v2 — what the docs don't tell you

Verified live before building on it. Four things would have broken a naive
implementation:

- The response is a **bare JSON array**, not an object wrapper.
- Elements arrive **out of order** — index by `originIndex`/`destinationIndex`,
  never by position.
- `distanceMeters` is **omitted when zero** rather than sent as 0, so a missing
  field means zero, not "unroutable."
- `duration` is a **string** (`"1244s"`).
- Costs are genuinely **asymmetric** (A→B 1244 s vs B→A 1234 s), which is why
  both rectangular matrices are required rather than merely prudent.

Scoring runs **server-side**, not in the browser Maps JS class: it reuses the
existing `GOOGLE_GEOCODING_KEY`, it lands in `api_cache` (7-day TTL, because
`TRAFFIC_UNAWARE` durations are deterministic), and it lets the action return one
fully-ranked list. Progressive rendering would have been actively harmful here —
since straight-line order in the dense band is noise, showing it first and
re-sorting would shuffle the list under the user's finger.

Unplanned nicety: changing only the detour budget re-fetches two of three
matrices; the vertex-to-vertex baseline stays cached because the route is
unchanged.

## Things only a browser found

Peer CODEX ran browser QA over four rounds and found **seven real defects** that
`npm run check`, the build, 122 unit tests and 43 DB tests all missed:

1. The suggester recommended **your own hotel** — anchor exclusion by
   route-proximity missed lodging on plans anchored elsewhere. Now excludes any
   place referenced as any plan's anchor, trip-wide.
2. Changing the detour budget **dropped the in-flight response but left stale
   rows painted** under the new label.
3. Date **Clear was a silent no-op**: `<input name="date">` preceded
   `<button name="date" value="">`, and `form.get('date')` returns the first
   value, so the input always won. Reported with the mechanism, not just the
   symptom.
4. A **dead Escape handler** — the fix had been written earlier but the patch
   matched the wrong tab depth and silently didn't apply.
5. The **empty-name error never rendered**, because `required` made the browser
   block submission before the server validation could run.
6. **Scroll-to-copy landed in the card body** — `block: 'center'` on a 3158px
   card in a 703px viewport necessarily puts the header ~1200px off-screen, and
   weather panels loading above shifted layout after the scroll.
7. **Select-all broke under pointer focus** (a regression from fix 5), so typing
   appended to the prefilled name: mousedown focuses and selects, mouseup
   collapses.

Two of those came with the mechanism attached, which meant going straight to the
fix. And #7 was caught only because the reviewer did a *readability* check for
#6 rather than trusting the rect maths — the corrupted title was visible in the
screenshot. A pass that only measured rectangles would have shipped a bug that
mangles the name on every duplicate.

Also worth recording: the reviewer refused to call #6 fixed when the card merely
*intersected* the viewport. It satisfied "scrolled into view" and was still wrong
for a person.

## Product decisions worth not re-litigating

- **Zero-cost rows read "on the way"**, not "+0 min".
- **Internal and external suggestions are separately sorted sections**, so a +1
  discovery can sit below a +2 saved place. User-curated places take precedence
  and the section labels make the ranking reset legible.
- **Cross-plan reuse is intentional.** A place already in another day plan stays
  inline with an "in <plan>" badge, not demoted or hidden. The owner: "I will
  build a new [plan] for a new day, but don't want the admin overhead of removing
  it from another. And who knows, maybe we'll go to the same place twice."
  Backed by data — Early Settlers Cemetery is in three plans, six more are in two,
  and 27 of 93 candidates for one plan are already used elsewhere.
- **The same place twice within one plan stays blocked.** Considered and declined.
- **Dates stay optional and out-of-range dates save**, with an advisory label.
  Days often get planned the morning of; dates matter for the handful of
  time-anchored commitments — a market that only runs Saturday, a booked dinner.
  That is why a dated place now pins in "Suggest stops" on its day.

## Cost shape of this trip

Blue Hill Thursday: on-the-way ×5, +1 ×2, +2 ×2 — then nothing until +19, +20,
+21, +24, +26, +28, +30. A tight village cluster and a distant tier, with almost
nothing between. So a 15-minute budget yields 9 options and 30 yields 19, and
widening further does little until ~20 minutes.
